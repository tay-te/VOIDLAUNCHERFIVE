# `expanse/` — VOID Expanse

A Fabric world-generation mod for **Minecraft 26.3** (the latest stable release, September 2026): thirty
biomes you won't find in vanilla, new kinds of tree, animals and wild life, 61 structures from wayside shrines
to walled citadels in every land biome, some 250 small hand-made finds scattered over the land and through
the caves, a world underground of its own, and taller mountains.

```
Fabric Loader ≥ 0.19.5 · Fabric API 0.161.0+26.3 · Java 25
```

## What's in it

### Biomes

Each biome takes the *variant* half of one vanilla biome's climate cell (see
`world/biome/BiomePlacement.java`). Vanilla's biome table has a second "variant" table for places where
the weirdness noise is positive, and most of its cells are empty. The Expanse biomes fill those empty
cells. Each one borders the vanilla biome it grew from. Two vanilla biomes give way, as the surface should look
like the real world: the pale garden becomes dark forest (Ancient Oakwood in its variant half) and the pink cherry
grove a meadow.

| Biome | Where it grows | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Woodland Glades** | temperate forest | spreading oaks and birches standing thick in places and thinning out into open glades of grass, daisies, poppies and cornflowers |
| **Ancient Oakwood** | dark forest | crooked old oaks with long mossy limbs, some ivy-clad, among dark oaks; ferns, bracken and moss over the ground and the boulders, fallen trunks with bracket fungus, a soft grey mist |
| **Redwood Giants** | cool wet taiga | redwoods 40–75 blocks tall with buttress roots and 2×2 and 3×3 trunks; fallen logs, mossy boulders, ferns, podzol |
| **Jade Karst** | hot and humid | Guilin-style limestone towers up to 76 blocks tall, with turf caps, bushes and hanging vines; jade-green ponds, bamboo, mist |
| **Red Dunes** | hot and dry | a sea of red sand in wind-shaped dunes (long windward slopes, steep lee faces), with arches of banded red rock standing out of them |
| **Willow Bayou** | swamp | weeping willows draped in Spanish moss; cattails, lily pads, standing water and mud; capybaras |
| **Amber Steppe** | savanna | golden grass, huge bottle-trunked baobabs and termite mounds |

Most sit within about 5 km of spawn on a typical seed. They also join the vanilla biome tags
of the biome they grew from, so villages, temples, mineshafts and mob variants appear in them the way
they would in that vanilla biome.

### Earth terrain

The overworld is made by a chunk generator of this mod's own, `expanse:earth`. It builds Earth-like land
from plate tectonics and erosion rather than from noise alone:

- **Tectonic plates.** The world is divided into plates about 4 km across (a jittered, domain-warped
  Voronoi diagram). Each plate is continental or oceanic, and continental plates cluster into continents.
  Each plate drifts, and what happens at a border depends on how the two plates move:
  - Two continents colliding raise a mountain range along the border.
  - A continent overriding an ocean plate raises a coastal range a little inland, above a trench.
  - Two ocean plates colliding throw up an island arc.
  - Ocean plates pulling apart leave a mid-ocean ridge on the sea floor.

  A continent with no ocean beside it gets an inland sea.
- **Erosion.** Each plate is worked out on a 32-block grid with the stream-power law of fluvial erosion,
  solved for its steady state:
  - Water drains from every point to the sea along a drainage tree, built by a priority flood from the
    coast with a gentle seaward tilt, so no river ever ends in a pit.
  - How much land drains through each point sets how steep the land there can stand. Small catchments
    stand steep (ridges, mountain flanks); great rivers run almost flat.
  - Heights are built up the tree from the sea, then the tree is rebuilt from the new heights, and the two
    are repeated until they agree.

  The results are sharp ridgelines, branching valleys and concave river profiles. Interior basins stay
  low, as plains, and old worn ranges cross the continents.
- **A tall world.** The Earth overworld runs from y -64 to 639, so the great ranges can stand 500 blocks and more
  over the sea. Long belts of high mountains cross the continents (a core of high peaks in broad foothills,
  bending and rising and falling along their length), besides the ranges where plates collide; the ice has ground
  their high valleys into troughs with broad floors. From the far view a wall of rock and snow stands behind the
  country in front of it.
- **The heartland.** Temperate country well inland and away from the ranges rolls rather than climbs: its small
  hills are worn lower over their valleys, and the land swells instead into long broad rises 20 to 40 blocks
  over the vales between them, a kilometre and more across, so ridge behind ridge stands against the sky. The
  mountains and the coasts keep their relief.
- **Escarpments.** Here and there in the temperate countries the upland ends in a long edge, a cliff 30 to 55
  blocks over the country below, winding for kilometres (the cuestas of the sandstone and limestone country);
  rivers cut their way through. The rims and the level tops of the lone volcanic hills are marked in the column
  (`Column.site`) for VOID Hearth's castles.
- **Volcanoes** are eroded, not turned: each cone's foot wanders in and out, one flank stands fuller than
  another, and its gullies wind down it as water cuts them.
- **Countries** (`world/terrain/Countries.java`). Over the relief lies a map of countries, regions some five
  kilometres across, so a journey crosses from one country into the next rather than from patch to patch of
  biome. Each reshapes its land its own way, after the Bohemia of the old maps:
  - *Heartland:* rolling loess country, its hills worn lower still over broad vales; mostly fields, woods on
    the rises.
  - *Rock city:* sandstone country where the land has weathered into towns of rock, blocks and towers 16 to
    50 blocks high split by corridors two to four wide, forest on their tops (Bohemian Paradise, Adršpach).
    Its rock is sandstone through and through, in beds.
  - *Volcanic hills:* rolling country studded with lone bell-shaped hills, 45 to 115 blocks high, the old
    volcanoes' cores, basalt showing on the steeper ones; one in four has a level top where a castle would
    stand (the České středohoří). Rivers flow round them.
  - *Lake district:* land the ice left: scoured low, swarms of drumlins all lying one way, a lake in every
    cell and a great lake in every one that will hold one.
  - *Gorges:* a limestone plateau, level-topped and dimpled with sinkholes, its rivers sunk in steep-walled
    gorges 30 to 45 deep (the Moravian Karst).
  - *Old forest:* broad, rounded uplands, the worn roots of old mountains, mostly forest (Šumava).

  Which kind a country is follows the climate at its heart: the Bohemian countries in the temperate zone,
  glacial lakes and old forest in the cold, gorges and volcanic hills here and there in the warm; the steppe,
  deserts and tropics keep their own landforms. Neighbouring countries grade into each other over about a
  kilometre. `./gradlew terrainPreview -Pcountries=true` tints them on the map.
- **Waterfalls.** Where the land breaks off in a face of rock (an escarpment, a gorge wall, a rock town, a crag)
  a spring now and then breaks out under the rim, and its water falls the height of the face in a white ribbon
  (`world/feature/CliffFallsFeature.java`).
- **Meanders.** Rivers swing either side of their course, and in some country twice as wide, looping through their
  vales.
- **Cliffs are rock.** Wherever the ground is steeper than soil will lie (some 55 degrees), the soil is stripped and
  the rock shows: stone, or the country's own sandstone, limestone or basalt. A gorge wall or a hill's crag is a
  face of rock, not a staircase of dirt with grass on every step.
- **Rivers that reach the sea.** Wherever enough land drains through a point there is a river. Each one:
  - widens downstream, from 3-block brooks to great lowland rivers nearly 50 blocks across, in broad
    flat-floored vales half a dozen times as wide;
  - follows smooth curves between grid points, wandering either side;
  - holds real water above sea level, falling stretch by stretch toward the sea in small rapids;
  - sits in a valley floor with firm banks and a gravel, sand and clay bed, whose sides spread wider the
    deeper the river lies below the land round it, so a valley climbs out at a slope you can walk rather
    than standing as a wall over the floor.

  Where a river runs near the border with another plate, its valley carries on across the border too,
  fading out over 170 blocks. Each plate carves only its own rivers, so without this a valley's side would
  stop dead at the seam and stand there as a wall.
- **Lakes** (`world/terrain/TerrainModel.java`). There is one at most in each 560-block cell (three in five have one),
  about 20 in a 6 km square. Each sits in a quiet hollow of open country: well inland, away from rivers,
  volcanoes and plate borders, outside the dry country (whose basins are salt flats) and the wet tropics.
  - The water stands a block below the lowest point of its rim, so the lake holds itself in. The shore is
    built up a little wherever it dips under the water, and the generator banks it with earth.
  - The shoreline bends into bays and points. The bed shelves from the shore into a bowl, and the bigger
    lowland lakes have islands with shoals round them.
  - High up a lake is a small deep tarn.
  - A lake takes the biome of the country round it. Reeds and lily pads come to its shores as they do to
    rivers', since those test for the water itself. No cave opens under one.
- **Great lakes.** Here and there (a few in a 12 km square, at most one in each 1400-block cell, never where it
  is dry, tropical, volcanic or near a plate border) a lowland river valley lies flooded behind a moraine the ice left across
  it. The water stands five or six blocks over the river, and the lake reaches up the valley (up to 700 blocks
  from its middle) until the land upstream has risen out of it, so it runs a kilometre and more and sprawls
  out along every side valley, with drowned arms, headlands and islands where hilltops stand clear.
  - Toward the rim the shallower ground is built up into a broad moraine plug across the valley, with a notch
    where the river spills out; its outer face falls away into the valley below. The rim's reach wavers
    with the bearing, so no shore is ever an arc of a circle.
  - The river comes in at the head and goes on from the outlet; under the water it is the lake's.
  - Two great lakes never overlap; the site search is done once per cell for every thread, so the lakes
    cost the terrain next to nothing.
- **The climate follows the land.** The air warms and cools over broad zones tens of kilometres across; the rain
  comes in off the sea on a westerly wind, so the coasts are wet and the land dries the further it lies inland, and
  a great range wrings the rain out of the wind, leaving the country in its lee dry for tens of kilometres (the rain
  shadow) while its windward foothills are the wettest of all. So the steppe lies behind the mountains and the
  green heartland where the rain falls: every country and biome lies where it does for a reason
  (`TerrainModel.climateAt`; `terrainPreview -Pclimate=true` shows it).
- **Biomes follow the land.** The climate values the biome source reads come from the terrain:
  - continentalness from distance to the coast;
  - erosion from how mountainous the land is;
  - peaks and valleys from height above the nearest river.

  Temperature falls with altitude. Beaches meet the sea, river biomes follow the rivers, and peaks get
  peak biomes. Temperature and humidity are computed by the terrain model too (vanilla's noises and
  proportions), so the land knows its own climate.
- **Landforms that bring their own biome.** Some land is shaped by more than rivers, and the shape decides
  the biome (`world/terrain/Landform.java`). The terrain model tags those columns with a weirdness nothing
  else reaches, and the biome source holds a point there for each, so they are ordinary biomes to locate,
  structures and features:
  - **Volcanic Highlands:** stratovolcanoes along the arcs where a continent rides over an ocean plate,
    on the hotspot islands, and now and then alone far inland. Their uplift goes into the erosion model,
    so rivers drain off them like real ones, and a cone with radial gullies stands on top. Some craters
    hold lava, some a lake. The upper slopes are ash and tuff, with basalt flows edged with obsidian and
    fumaroles breathing smoke.
  - **Painted Canyons:** dry uplands terraced into mesas and buttes, with every cliff banded like the
    badlands and the rivers sunk in sheer canyons.
  - **Salt Flats:** in arid lowlands, dead-flat white salt crust with pink brine pools and salt chimneys.
  - **Tepuis:** in the wet tropics, sheer table mountains standing out of the forest. Their tops are a
    misty lost world, and springs pour down the cliffs. The lower part of every cliff is buried in a talus
    apron, the rock it has shed: steepest against the wall, spreading concave into the forest, a fifth to
    a half of the way up, rising in spurs and dipping into gullies along the foot. Each tepui is made whole
    from the climate at its centre, so the edge of the wet tropics never cuts one off along a line.
- **A world underground.** Under the Earth terrain the underground is the Expanse's own: no vanilla noise
  caves, no carvers, no vanilla cave biomes. (Other world types, such as Large Biomes, Amplified or a world
  with the Earth pack switched off, keep vanilla's caves with their lush caves, dripstone and deep dark.)
  Beneath the land runs a network to follow (it is not open space everywhere), from
  small passages to great tunnels to realms, landscapes of their own under a stone sky:
  - **Ways in:** cave mouths at the foot of hillsides and river banks, an adit running level into the hill
    and then down; sinkholes, funnels over a shaft that drops into a chamber; ravines, where a fissure
    breaks the surface. None opens under the sea, in a river or a lake, or on a volcano.
  - **Passages:** three levels of winding, branching passages (a sparse network of links over a jittered
    grid, so they fork, loop and end): a shallow one 30–50 blocks down that climbs with the land under
    mountains, one at the depth of the great tunnels that bends to meet them wherever they pass, and a
    deep one that comes out in the walls of the realms. Some junctions open into chambers 20–90 blocks
    across; a few deep ones sink into bowls of lava.
  - **Fissures:** tall narrow cracks through all the levels, some down into the lava.
  - **Great tunnels:** two families, a few hundred blocks apart, 12–24 blocks wide and up to 34 high under
    arched roofs, rising and falling between y 12 and 44.
  - **River tunnels:** one family carries rivers, with a channel down the middle, a dry ledge either side
    and waterfalls where the level drops.
  - **Realms:** about one every 1.5 km, 110 to 440 blocks across, deep in the deepslate under vaults up to
    130 high. A dry tunnel comes out high on a realm's wall with a scree ramp down from its mouth; a river
    pours out of its tunnel in a waterfall, into a plunge pool it has scoured. Below:
    - hills, ridges and valleys, or terraced mesas, rising in rubble slopes to the walls;
    - lakes in the low ground, and lava seas in the deepest realms;
    - stalagmite mountains, the tallest reaching the roof as pillars;
    - inverted peaks hanging from the vault, and great hourglass columns;
    - a plateau in the middle, where an underground structure may stand.
  - **Twelve characters, after the land above:** a realm takes its character from the climate, the sea,
    the mountains and the volcanoes over it (with chance enough that any can turn up anywhere), and each has
    a biome of its own (its music, creatures, fog and motes):
    - *Glowcap Wilds:* moss hills under a forest of giant mushrooms, lit by the shroomlight in their gills.
    - *Verdant Hollow:* moss, azaleas, clay ponds of dripleaf, spore blossoms, vines hanging thick.
    - *Stalagmite Deeps:* stalagmite fields and hanging spires, terraced flowstone, still pools.
    - *Geode Vaults:* terraced mesas of calcite and tuff, crusted with amethyst and glowing prismite.
    - *Ember Deeps:* the deepest, basalt and magma shores round a sea of lava.
    - *Sunless Mere:* an underground lake with islands and stacks, beaches, and sea pickles on its bed.
    - *Rime Hollows* (under snow country): a frozen lake, snow and packed and blue ice, ice spikes,
      icicles hanging from an ice-clad vault, frostblooms glowing in the snow.
    - *Root Hollows* (under forests): giant deeproot roots coming down out of the vault and arching across
      it, rooted earth and moss, ferns and firefly bushes, hanging roots and glowroots, lily-padded ponds.
    - *Fossil Deeps* (under deserts and dry country): dry sand and sandstone mesas and dunes, hoodoos,
      the bones of giants lying half buried, suspicious sand to brush round them.
    - *Tidal Grottos* (near the coasts): flowstone terraces stepping down to a still salt lake, tide pools on
      every step with sea pickles, seagrass and coral, kelp in the lake, glowworm silk hanging from the roof.
    - *Amber Hollows* (under conifer woods and the steppe): glowing amber with insects caught in it, amber
      spikes and boulders, resin, honeycomb, and pools the colour of honey.
    - *Brimstone Vaults* (under volcanoes and mountains): basalt causeways, sulfur and its spikes, hot
      springs whose potent sulfur erupts in geysers, lava welling up in vents.
  - **Cave systems of their own** (`world/terrain/CaveSystems.java`, lined by `CaveSystemsLife.java`),
    each a shape no other cave has, from common to rare:
    - *Lava tubes* (every volcano has a few): three to six smooth tubes radiating down a volcano's flanks
      a few blocks under the ground, round vaults over a flat floor of smooth basalt with magma and
      obsidian in it. Where the thin roof fell in, a skylight opens to the sky and the land above has
      slumped in under it, grass and ferns growing in the light.
    - *Maze caves* (uncommon, in patches a few hundred blocks across): a labyrinth cut along the joints of
      the rock at the shallow passages' depth, narrow tall passages crossing at right angles every ten
      blocks or so, pinched shut at the top, some joints sealed so they loop and dead-end, a small room
      where a few cross. Their walls are the beds the joints were dissolved in, gently dipping: limestone
      under the wet warm lands, sandstone under the dry, elsewhere flowstone-brown dripstone with white
      seams of calcite and beds of tuff. Dripstone hangs off the roofs and glow lichen marks the way.
    - *Abysses* (rare: two in five 2.4 km cells): a shaft 20–50 blocks across falling from open ground
      150 blocks and more to a pool, its walls swelling and pinching and ringed with ledges every twenty-odd
      blocks, grassed near the light and mossed below. Vines hang down the upper walls, glow lichen
      lines the deep ones, and a spring pours off the lip.
    - *Rift halls* (rare): an underground canyon 150–320 blocks long, a few blocks wide and 70–120 high, its
      walls leaning together overhead and boulders wedged across it high up. The walls are banded with the
      tilted beds it split (deepslate, tuff, dark basalt, thin white calcite), and glow lichen and glowworm
      silk light it all the way up.
    - *Geodes* (rare, deep, y −36 to −14): a hollow egg 18–38 blocks across, amethyst (some budding)
      inside calcite inside smooth basalt, clusters growing in from every wall, and giant crystal beams
      crossing the hollow, every third of glowing prismite. Where a deep tunnel broke into one, the crust
      frays out over the tunnel's walls; elsewhere a geode is found only by mining.

    `-Pslice=plan` marks them (lava tubes orange, skylights yellow, mazes purple, rifts cyan, geodes pink,
    abysses black) and prints where each is.
  - **The caves between** take after their own biomes (Mossgrown Caves, Dripstone Grottos, Echoing
    Depths and Sulfur Seeps, where vanilla's cave biomes would be; `world/biome/EarthBiomeSource.java` swaps
    them in for the Earth generator alone) or after the land above: snow and icicles
    under the cold lands, sand under the deserts, moss under the wet ones, bare stone elsewhere, with glow
    lichen.
  - **Light:** glowcaps, glow berries, shroomlight, sea pickles, prismite, amber, glowroots, glowworm silk,
    frostblooms, magma, glow lichen and lava light the realms in places, so they are a dark land of lit
    places rather than a black void.
  - **Dry:** the aquifers flood nothing (through their `exclusion`), so the water and lava lie where the
    model puts them; below y -54 every open space is lava, as in vanilla.
  - **Safety:** apart from the ways in (and the abysses and lava-tube skylights, which are ways in too),
    nothing comes within 16 blocks of the ground above or lies under the sea.
  - **Preview:** `./gradlew terrainPreview -Pslice=plan` maps it from above (realm floors hill-shaded and
    tinted by character, passages by level, chambers, fissures, ways in and sinkholes) and prints the
    realms by character; `-Pslice=side -Pcx=… -Pcz=…` draws a section through the ground; `-Pslice=<y>`
    cuts through everything at one height; `-Pslice=cavebench` times the cavern model per column.
- **Vanilla where vanilla is good.** Aquifers, ore veins, surface rules and structures are vanilla's,
  running on the new terrain (the generator wraps vanilla's noise generator).

It lives in a built-in data pack, **VOID Expanse: Earth**, **on by default**, which you can switch off in
*Create World → More → Data Packs* to get vanilla terrain with Expanse biomes. The pack also raises the
overworld's build limit to **y=384**, for mountain ranges that need the room.

> The Earth terrain changes how the overworld is made, so use it on **new worlds**. Don't add it to a
> world that was created without it.

`./gradlew terrainPreview -Pradius=8192 -Pstep=16` renders the model straight to
`build/terrain_preview.png` in a few seconds, without starting the game. The F3 screen shows the plate,
uplift, distance to the coast and the nearest river.

**Natural trees for everything** (`tools/worldgen_trees.py`). Vanilla's trees are replaced in the world it
generates, in vanilla's biomes and the Expanse's alike. Each kind has a family of natural shapes that mixes the
ages as real woods do: young trees, mature ones, now and then a veteran or a giant, a dead snag still standing,
vanilla's fallen trees.

- **Oak:** forks low into crooked limbs under clumps of leaves. In woods it is tall with climbing limbs; in
  fields and on hills it is low and spreading. Old veterans have a flared foot and bracket fungus.
- **Birch:** a slim white stem, leaning a little, with a light drooping crown.
- **Spruce:** tall slim cones, their lower trunks bare where the shade killed the branches. Old ones stand over
  the wood, and the old-growth taigas have two-wide giants.
- **Pine:** the Scots pine, a tall bare trunk with a flat crown up in the light; young pines.
- **Dark oak:** the tall dense dark oaks, old forked ones with bracket fungus, young ones.
- **Acacia:** umbrellas of flat pads on forked trunks.
- **Jungle:** tall forked trees with storeyed crowns, and buttressed giants over the canopy; the cloud forest's are
  hung with Spanish moss.
- **Cherry:** spreading crowns of blossom.
- **Swamp:** spreading oaks hung with vines.

How it works: vanilla's tree mixes (`trees_plains`, `trees_taiga`, `dark_forest_vegetation` and the rest) are
overridden with every tree swapped for its family. The few biomes that plant one tree directly (`trees_cherry`,
`trees_swamp`) have that placement overridden. Each replacement keeps the chance, the bees, the leaf litter and the
vines of the tree it replaces. Pale oak (the creaking's heart grows in it), mangroves, jungle bushes and the fallen
trees stay vanilla's. Saplings still grow vanilla's trees, so tree farms are unchanged.

Every tree has a full wood set: log, wood, stripped log and wood, planks, stairs, slab, fence, gate,
door, trapdoor, button and pressure plate, plus leaves and a sapling. All are craftable, strippable,
burnable, and work as fuel.

### Wild life

Small ambient creatures that make the land feel lived in (`entity/life/`, `world/LivingWorld.java`). Like
vanilla's bat they are scenery rather than livestock: they turn up around whoever is there to see them
and leave once nobody is.

| Creature | Where and when | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Butterflies** (monarch, cabbage white, morpho, brimstone, red admiral) | By day, near flowers, on any land where rain falls (or savanna) at temperature 0.5 and up | Drift about a patch of flowers, settle on blooms with their wings slowly opening and closing, flutter off if you walk up (sneak to watch). Settle for the night and the rain. |
| **Moths** (luna, tiger) | The same places, by night | Flutter round lanterns and torches. |
| **Dragonflies** (azure, emerald, scarlet) | By day over open water, temperature 0.5 and up | Dart and hover over a stretch of river or pond they keep to, never more than a couple of blocks up; rest on the reeds and the bank. Glassy wings. |
| **Songbirds** (robin, bluebird, goldfinch, cardinal, sparrow; the snow bunting in the cold) | By day on any land where rain falls, or savanna | Little flocks that hop and peck on the grass and sit on treetops, fences and walls singing; they flit off a few blocks now and then, and burst up together with an alarm call if you walk within five blocks (two, sneaking). Roost at night. |
| **Herons and egrets** | By day in the shallows and on the banks of rivers and lakes, temperature 0.3 and up (egrets in warm country) | Stand in the water and stalk, now and then striking at fish; lift off with a croak and slow wingbeats, neck folded and legs trailing, if you come within twelve blocks, and come down further along the water. |
| **Owls** (tawny, barn; the snowy owl in the snow) | At night on treetops in the woods (`#expanse:habitat/owls`: forests, taigas, jungles, swamps, the snow) | Hoot, turn their heads to follow you, blink, and drift silently to another tree if you come close. If one is still about by day it dozes, eyes shut. |

Each has its own voice where it should: birdsong, alarm calls, owl hoots, heron croaks and wingbeats,
synthesised by `tools/gen_living.py`. Spawn eggs for all of them are in the creative tab.

**How they appear.** Vanilla spawns ambient mobs at a random height and caps them at 15 per player, shared
with the bats in the caves, which would leave a butterfly a rare sight a hundred blocks off. So once a
second, for each player, a small spawner looks at a couple of surface columns 20 to 48 blocks away, reads
the ambient spawn list there (the same biome data vanilla reads) and places the first of these creatures
whose own rules the spot meets (flowers for butterflies, shallows for herons, a treetop for an owl), up to
16 within 48 blocks (8 butterflies, 6 dragonflies, 10 songbirds, 2 herons, 3 owls). They leave once no
player is within 72 blocks, and slip away out of their hours when nobody is near enough to see.

**Where, by tag and climate.** Every spawn is added through Fabric's biome modifications by biome tag or
climate, never by naming a biome, so new biomes are covered as they join vanilla's tags or simply by
their temperature and rainfall. The same goes for vanilla's own creatures, which the Earth terrain's
rivers need in more places than vanilla puts them:

- **Fish up every river.** Rivers cross every biome, but vanilla's fish only live in the river and ocean
  biomes. Salmon (and some cod) join the cool and temperate land biomes, tropical fish the hot ones. Vanilla
  only lets them spawn at or below sea level, and the Earth terrain's rivers run above it, so they may also
  spawn in the top 13 blocks of any water open to the sky (`SurfaceWaterSpawnMixin`).
- **Frogs by the water** in the wet land biomes and the river biome. Outside the swamps
  (`#expanse:habitat/frogs_anywhere`) they spawn only within three blocks of water (`FrogSpawnMixin`), so
  they line the banks rather than the fields.
- **Turtles** on jungle and mangrove sandbars, **rabbits** in the plains and woods, **foxes** in the
  broadleaf woods: places vanilla leaves without them.
- **Fireflies.** Vanilla's firefly bush makes the fireflies (at night, or in deep shade). Clusters of them
  now line the banks of every temperate and warm river and lake, and grow under the canopy of wet woods.

### Woods and open country

Every natural tree asks the land whether a tree would stand there (`expanse:land_fit`,
`TerrainModel.woodland`), so from a hill, or from the far view, the land reads as it does in Bohemia: dark woods
on the rises, light open country between.

- A broad noise lays out woods and clearings with edges a dozen blocks wide, so a wood ends rather than thinning
  out; woods keep to the rises and slopes and leave the vale floors and floodplains open.
- Each country has its own share of open land: the heartland mostly fields, the old forest and the rock towns
  mostly wood. Above about y 185 the trees give out.
- **Hedgerows** divide the open country into fields wherever it lies: on the Earth terrain in the open land of
  the temperate forests and meadows too, never inside a wood.
- **Fields.** Inside the hedges each field is farmed as a whole: ripe wheat gold from end to end, a field being
  reaped (stubble, the sheaves stood up in stooks in rows), green wheat, fallow earth turned over, hay meadow grown
  tall round its haystack, or pasture with a flock of sheep or a few cattle grazing it. From a hill the vale below is a quilt of gold and
  green and brown, under the dark woods on the rises.
- **The greens** of the temperate land are muted and olive, as in a real countryside: vanilla's temperate biomes
  take greens of their own (`world/biome/Palette.java`) instead of the colour map's bright lime.
- **Lone trees** (`expanse:hilltop`): a veteran oak on the crest of an open rise, a mark on the skyline seen for
  miles.

`./gradlew terrainPreview -Pwoods=true` shows the woods dark and the open country pale.

### Ground cover and little things

The land between structures should never be empty. Two layers fill it:

- **Ground cover** (`world/feature/Foliage.java`) thickens the vanilla biomes where vanilla's is thin:
  undergrowth and leaf litter in forests and taigas, wildflower drifts on the plains, dry grass on the
  savannas, spruce shrubs in the snow. Every river bank in every biome gets the plants of the water's
  edge: reeds, cattails, sugar cane, lily pads, clay and seagrass. Rivers cross every biome on the Earth
  terrain, so this can't be left to the river biome. Low banks are levelled with the water so cane can
  root.
- **The understory** (`tools/worldgen_foliage.py`, in every biome by its habitat, vanilla's and the
  Expanse's alike) gives the countryside the layered green of Bohemia in Kingdom Come: Deliverance II.
  Its density follows vanilla's flower noise, so there are open stretches and thickets rather than an
  even carpet. By habitat:
  - *Woods:* thickets of hazel, elder, dogwood and hawthorn under the trees; ferns, bush and grass over
    the floor in drifts; bramble patches.
  - *Conifers:* juniper and the odd hazel; ferns, moss and bilberry.
  - *Meadows:* waist-high grass in swathes with wildflowers through it, lone shrubs and thickets.
  - *Jungles:* bushes and ferns. *Dry country:* dry grass and dead bushes.
  - *Water's edge:* young alders, weeping willows and birches along every temperate river and lake.
  - *Hedgerows* (`world/feature/HedgerowFeature.java`): across farmed country (about half of the plains)
    they divide the land into fields of a few acres. A shrub stands every other block along each field
    edge, with the odd oak left standing in the hedge, a gateway in every stretch and some stretches
    gone. The field grid wavers and each row is offset, so the hedges meet in T-junctions and never run
    ruler-straight.
  - *Fishponds* (`world/feature/FishpondFeature.java`), as the Bohemian countryside is strewn with them, in
    the temperate woods and meadows (vanilla's and the Expanse's). Each is a shallow pond dug into gently
    falling ground, held by an earth dam across its lower end. A wooden sluice tower (the "monk") stands in
    the water by the dam, with a plank walk out to it from the grassed crest. The reeds, lily pads, alders and
    willows come to its banks as they come to any water.
  - *Wayside sights* among the little things of the fields and woods, alongside the wayside crosses,
    milestones and hunters' high seats already there:
    - squat stone conciliation crosses;
    - field chapels: a whitewashed niche under a tiled gable, a candle on its altar;
    - old lime trees at the field corners, each with a bench under it.
- **Little things** (`world/feature/LittleThings.java`, the `expanse:template` feature) are 183
  hand-made templates set into the ground, in 99 regional groups. Every land biome has its own set, in
  its own wood, stone and soil, in three tiers:
  - **Natural things**, one every one or two chunks (46 sets). Stumps and fallen trees in each local wood:
    a birch snag stepped with bracket fungus, a maple down in its own leaves, an oak torn up by the roots
    with its root plate on edge, a hollow redwood you can walk through, the buttress roots of a fallen
    kapok, a split old olive stump, aspens gnawed to a point by beavers, a grey beech gone hollow. Erratics,
    mossy boulder fields, scree, standing stones and a dolmen on the hills. Ant mounds in the taiga,
    sphagnum hummocks with cotton grass in the muskeg, toadstools on the mushroom fields, a pale snag
    hung with pale moss in the pale garden. In the dry country termite mounds (red cathedral mounds in the
    outback), granite and sandstone tors, hoodoos and rockfalls in the canyons, dry washes, salt ridges
    and a drying brine pool on the salt flats. Lava bombs, obsidian shards, sulphur-crusted fumaroles
    and ash-killed snags on the volcanic highlands. Ice boulders, avalanche debris and ice shards in the
    high snow, a frost-split prismite geode. Rock pools on the tepui, tide pools, whale bones, kelp
    wrack and driftwood on the shores, coral rubble on the coral coast, snags standing in swamp water,
    mangrove root tangles.
  - **Traces of people**, one every four or five chunks (31 sets). A scarecrow over a weedy vegetable
    patch, bee hives under a hedge, a bird box, a wayside cross, a milestone, a picnic left on its
    blanket, a cart down a wheel; a charcoal burner's clamp, a hunter's high seat and a saw trestle in the
    woods, a sugaring kettle in the maples; a stone sheepfold, peat drying in ricks and a shepherd's
    shelter on the moor; a lean-to and a springboard stump in the north, a drifted sled and an
    ice-fishing hole in the snow, trail marker poles on the passes; an upturned boat, net racks and a
    runestone by the fjords; a thorn boma, a yurt ring, an ovoo and a balbal on the steppe, a drover's
    swag camp and a stockyard in the outback, a ranch gate, an ocotillo fence and a prospector's pit in
    the desert; salt cairns and stacks, a sulphur pit, a fallen column with amphorae and a lavender
    terrace in the olive groves; a swamped skiff, a fish weir and stilt stubs in the swamps, stone idols
    in the jungle and stone lanterns in the karst. A few hold a lost cache, a sled's barrel or a
    prospector's barrel.
  - **Ruins**, one every ten to fourteen chunks (21 sets, 25 templates, 7 to 14 blocks across): the corner
    of a cottage with its chimney fallen across the floor, a chapel gable with its empty bell-cote and the
    bell in the grass, a collapsed lime kiln, a farmstead's footings and well, an overgrown garden wall
    and gate, a moss-swallowed statue, a toppled timber watch-post, a snowed-in trapper's cabin, a fallen
    boathouse, wrecks on the beaches, a sunken stair to a broken doorway on the peaks and the tepui, a

  - Roughly one chunk in 48 hides a half-buried lost chest.

  Each piece surveys its footprint before it lands. It refuses water, steep ground, logs and other
  little things, and is bedded into the lowest column so nothing floats. The ruins stay on gentler ground

**How they find a place.** The feature is handed a column and finds the caves in it itself: it scans down for
floors (rock with open air above and a roof over it) in its biomes, read where the thing would stand, so the
realms' own biomes work although they are not in the biome source. A template then goes in only where every
column of its footprint has natural ground within a block or two of the floor and the air is clear to its top,
so its height decides where it can go: a lamp fits any passage, a survey tower only a realm. Pieces cut into the
wall look for the cave wall at head height, turn to face out of it, and need solid rock all round the room
(one block beyond it), so a cell never breaks into another cave. Nothing goes within a few blocks of a structure
piece or of anything built (a mineshaft's planks, a dungeon's cobblestone, another cave thing). The cave
decoration (`CavernLife`) only works natural ground, so it grows round them.

The biome groups the shared palettes use are in `tools/structures/regions.py`.

**Villages.** Seven regional villages, built on vanilla's own village machinery. They use the same pools,
jigsaw names, terrain-following streets, beds, bells, job sites, golems and cats, so villagers live,
work, trade, breed and get raided exactly as in a plains village. They join `#minecraft:village`, so
`/locate structure #minecraft:village` and explorer maps find them, and 1 in 50 is a zombie village.

| Village | Biome | Villagers | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glades (`village_wisteria`) | Woodland Glades | plains | Dark oak timber frames and plaster under tile roofs, an apple orchard with beehives |
| Redwood | Redwood Giants | taiga | Log cabins, a sawpit, a smokehouse, woodpiles |
| Coast | Palm Coast | jungle | Thatched palm houses on stilts, a fishing jetty, drying racks, beached boats |

Every Expanse biome has its villager type set (`world/ExpanseVillagers.java`), so a villager spawned,
cured or (half the time, as in vanilla) born in a Jade Karst takes the jungle look.

Structures choose their sites the way a builder would (`expanse:sited_jigsaw`,
`world/SitedJigsawStructure.java`): never on water (unless it's a stilt house or a jetty), on a cliff edge
or at the bottom of a gully. Small sights keep clear of anywhere a big structure or a village could stand,
and of vanilla's villages, outposts, mansions, temples, witch huts, igloos, trail ruins, abandoned camps,
ruined portals and wrecks; the buildings that share vanilla's biomes keep clear of those too. Trees,
rock pillars, ice spires, arches and dunes in turn keep clear of every structure piece
(`clear_of_structures` placement filter, `world/StructureClearance.java`).

### Partners

Settlements, castles, worked buildings and the roads between them are VOID Hearth's. The Expanse keeps to
the land between them and stays off any land a settlement has planned. `dev.voidmc.expanse.api.LandClaims`
is how it knows: a partner registers a `(level, x, z) -> claimed` lookup, and every Expanse thing that covers
more than a block asks it first. That covers the little things and wayside sights, the hedgerows and the
fishponds. With Hearth installed, Hearth's villages stand in for the Expanse's own (Hearth leaves the
village structure sets out); played alone, the Expanse keeps its seven village styles.

The same goes for the Expanse's other peopled and working places: the stilt hamlet, palm harbour, caravanserai,
logging camp, miners' camps (and the cavern mine camp), terrace farm, tea garden, wisteria manor, cloud
monastery, karst pagoda, lighthouse, frost outpost, nomad camp, beach hut and bayou shack. Hearth builds living
versions of them, so with Hearth installed each stands down (`"peopled": true` on its structure,
`SitedJigsawStructure`): a set it shares with wild sights tries those in its place, a set of its own drops out of
the world, and `/locate` finds none at once. The land between towns keeps everything else: the camps, wells,
shrines, ruins, treehouses, bothies and lodges. `-Dexpanse.dev.hearth=true` shows a world as it would be with
Hearth.

With Hearth, and only then, the wild holds the traces of folk who left instead, rarer than the wild sights (one
grid every 40 chunks, `abandoned`): an **abandoned mine camp** (both palettes, the ore still showing at the face, for
Hearth's prospectors to report), a **collapsed hamlet** on its stilts, a **burnt-out logging camp**. Each is the living
place's own pieces run through a ruin (`tools/structures/abandoned.py`, written with the rest by gen_structures.py):
beds, workstations, lights, glass and doors gone, the roofs fallen in the more the higher they stood, tents rotted,
moss on the boards and the floors; the burnt camp charred to basalt and coal, its boards holed, its grass gone to
ash. No animals are left in them. Every surface structure, these included, keeps off land Hearth has planned
(`LandClaims`), checked over the whole of what it would build.

On the VOID Atlas map an Expanse world's places carry their own names rather than plain biome names
(`client/atlas`, the `"atlas"` entrypoint, called only when Atlas is installed). They are made up from the
seed and the place's position, so every client calls a place the same, and the words follow the country:
the temperate heartland is Bohemian (Brezek Forest, Lake Maletz, the Olsava), the cold north Norse
(Hamarvik Barrens, Ulfdal Cleft), dry and tropical land southern (Zaroma Desert). A world counts as an
Expanse world when its biomes include the Expanse's; elsewhere Atlas names its places itself. The build
compiles against stand-ins for Atlas's API (`src/atlasApi`), never shipped, so the Expanse needs nothing of
Atlas to build or run.

### For other mods

`dev.voidmc.expanse.api.ExpanseCountry.sampler(level).at(x, z)` says what the land is like at a place (its
country, humidity, temperature, how low it lies, how far from water and the sea, its height, and a hint at the
air's character) for mods that dress it, such as VOID Gloam's mist and haze. It is a pure function of the
seed and the coordinates, cheap enough to ask every tick.

`TerrainModel.sampleFar(x, z, out)` gives a far view (VOID LOD's horizon past 32 km) the land averaged over cells of
`TerrainModel.FAR_GRID` (512) blocks and up: the same plates, drainage, climate and countries, each plate worked out
again on a 512-block grid (some 30 KB, cached apart), so a 100 km horizon never builds a full plate. It agrees with
`sample` to within about 7 blocks over a cell on average, at a few microseconds a call (`terrainPreview
-Pslice=far` measures it). `TerrainModel.sample` is what LOD reads nearer in; `reservePlates` lets it keep more
plates built while it does.

### Extras

- `/expanse atlas [radius] [blocksPerPixel]` (needs operator level 2) writes a shaded biome-and-elevation
  map of the area to `<world>/expanse_atlas/`. It samples the generator directly, so it covers ground
  nobody has explored yet.
- Advancements: entering any Expanse biome starts a new advancement tab. *Wanderer of the Expanse* is a
  challenge for visiting every Expanse climate biome, and *Shaped by Fire and Ice* one for the five
  landforms of the Earth terrain.

## Build

You need **JDK 25**. The Gradle wrapper (Gradle 9.7) is committed, so use it rather than a system
Gradle.

```sh
cd expanse
./gradlew build          # → build/libs/void-expanse-<version>.jar
```

To install, put the jar and Fabric API in `.minecraft/mods/`, using a Fabric 26.3 profile.

## Tooling

The JSON and the art are generated by scripts in `tools/`. The output is committed, so a normal build
doesn't need Python. Run the scripts again only when you change what they describe. Each script reads
the vanilla client jar that Loom caches after any build, and copies vanilla's own file formats from it
rather than from memory.

| Script | Writes |
|---|---|
| `tools/gen_assets.py` | Blockstates, models, item definitions, loot tables, recipes, recipe advancements, block and item tags, lang. Each block is cloned from the vanilla block it is shaped like (oak, tuff, sandstone, poppy…). |
| `tools/gen_worldgen.py` | Tree and terrain features, placements, the 13 biomes, surface rules, biome tags, advancements and the Earth terrain pack |
| `tools/gen_entities.py` | Entity geometry (`models/entity/*.json`, read at runtime by `JsonEntityModels`), the texture painted onto exactly that UV layout, and the entity loot tables |
| `tools/gen_living.py` | The wild creatures: their models and a skin per colour variant (on gen_entities.py's machinery), spawn egg sprites, loot, synthesised sounds (`sounds.json` and `.ogg`, via ffmpeg), the firefly features and the habitat biome tags |
| `tools/textures/gen_textures.py` | Every block and item texture, plus the mod icon, all drawn procedurally and deterministically |
| `tools/structures/gen_structures.py` | Every structure: templates (`.nbt`) built by `designs/*.py`, template pools, processor lists, structure sets, chest loot and tags. It validates its output against vanilla's block states and can render isometric previews (`--preview DIR`). |
| `tools/structures/gen_caves.py` | The things underground: cave templates (`structure/cave/`), their processor lists, loot, journals and the `expanse:cave_template` features, one per group in `CaveThings.java` (it checks the two agree). Validates with the structure validator; `--preview DIR` renders a contact sheet. |

### How this was checked without a monitor

- **Dedicated server smoke test.** Running the server with `-Dexpanse.dev.locate=6000
  -Dexpanse.dev.generate=true -Dexpanse.dev.atlas=8000:32 -Dexpanse.dev.stop=true` locates every Expanse
  biome (and, with `-Dexpanse.dev.structures=120`, every structure) and generates full chunks around each one, so every feature, tree and spawn rule runs. It then
  writes an atlas, logs the share of the map each biome covers, and shuts down (`command/DevHarness.java`).
  It also logs which cave biomes the world has (the Expanse's under the Earth terrain, vanilla's elsewhere),
  and `-Dexpanse.dev.visit=X,Z;X,Z` generates full chunks round chosen places and logs what grows there.
- **Client biome tour.** `src/gametest/` is a Fabric client game test. It creates a world, maps the biomes
  for 6 km round spawn, flies to the middle of each biome's largest patch (not its nearest sliver), and
  saves noon, ground-level and night screenshots. It then locates every structure and photographs it from
  whichever of eight directions has the clearest line of sight (`-Dexpanse.tour.only=a,b` limits it to the
  named structures), and stands back from the nearest volcano, canyon, salt pan, tepui and fjord to take
  each one whole. Run it headless with
  `SDL_VIDEO_FORCE_EGL=1 xvfb-run ./gradlew runClientGameTest`. The EGL setting is needed because 26.3
  creates its window with SDL3 and asks for an sRGB framebuffer, and Xvfb's GLX visuals don't offer one.
  With `-Dexpanse.dev.caves=true` it also generates the ground under spawn and round the nearest realm of each
  character and photographs the first of each underground thing that went in (`expanse_cave_*`), under night
  vision and as it is, and logs how many of each went in and how long the cave feature took. The tour reads
  `-D` options from the JVM it runs in; on a desktop, `JAVA_TOOL_OPTIONS="-Dexpanse.dev.caves=true" ./gradlew
  runClientGameTest` runs it in a window (`-Dexpanse.tour.biomes= -Dexpanse.tour.structures=false` and so on skip
  the other legs). `-Dexpanse.tour.systems=true` finds the nearest of each cave system
  (`-Dexpanse.tour.systems_reach=7200` searches further), logs the blocks lining it and photographs it from
  inside (an abyss from over its lip and from its pool).
